The ablation study was conducted to evaluate the individual contribution of three engineered features: 'Hillshade_Noon_to_3pm_Diff', 'Total_Horizontal_Distance', and 'Slope_x_Vertical_Distance_To_Hydrology'. The model used was a RandomForestClassifier, and performance was measured using 3-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation accuracy. Here's a summary of the results: * **Baseline Performance:** The model with all engineered features achieved an average accuracy of **0.9579**. * **Ablation Results:** * **Removing 'Hillshade_Noon_to_3pm_Diff':** The accuracy increased slightly to **0.9582**, a change of **+0.0002** from the baseline. This suggests that this feature, when removed, led to a minor improvement in performance. * **Removing 'Total_Horizontal_Distance':** The accuracy also increased slightly to **0.9582**, a change of **+0.0002** from the baseline. Similar to the previous feature, its removal resulted in a minor performance gain. * **Removing 'Slope_x_Vertical_Distance_To_Hydrology':** The accuracy showed the largest increase among the ablations, reaching **0.9584**, a change of **+0.0005** from the baseline. This indicates that this feature, when removed, led to the most significant (though still minor) performance improvement. **Conclusion:** The ablation study found that none of the individual engineered features ('Hillshade_Noon_to_3pm_Diff', 'Total_Horizontal_Distance', 'Slope_x_Vertical_Distance_To_Hydrology') positively contributed to the model's performance. In fact, removing each of these features individually led to a slight increase in accuracy, suggesting they might be introducing noise or redundancy rather than valuable information for this specific model and dataset. The feature 'Slope_x_Vertical_Distance_To_Hydrology' showed the largest (positive) change when removed, implying it was the least beneficial among the three.
